Chapter 37: AI-Powered Detection and Security Operations

An SOC agent should be judged by the evidence it preserves, the consequential actions it refuses to take, and the facts it notices or misses—not by how fluent its incident summary sounds. This chapter builds such an agent for a concrete workflow: enrich an identity alert, assemble a cited case brief, identify missing evidence, recommend a disposition, and request approval for containment. The agent may read narrowly scoped systems and draft work; it may not silently expand its investigation, change a control, disable an account, or convert uncertainty into fact.

The design separates three questions that are often collapsed. Did the agent retrieve the right evidence? Did it reason faithfully over that evidence? Was it authorized to perform the requested action? Better prompting cannot repair a missing audit source, and a well-grounded conclusion does not grant authority. Production safety depends on keeping those questions separate in data, policy, evaluation, and incident review.

The Case: Impossible Travel with a Plausible Explanation

An identity provider raises an impossible-travel alert for alice@example.com: Chicago at 14:02 UTC and Amsterdam at 14:17 UTC. A weak assistant paraphrases the alert and recommends account suspension. A useful investigator checks whether both events represent human sessions, whether an approved egress proxy explains one location, whether device identity and token lineage agree, and whether high-value actions followed. It then distinguishes observation, inference, and unknown.

The desired output is not merely “benign” or “malicious.” It is a compact evidence packet: a timeline whose factual claims cite immutable evidence identifiers; explicit coverage of required questions; an omission list for unavailable or unexamined evidence; a calibrated disposition; proposed actions checked against policy; and the model, prompt, retriever, policy, and tool versions needed for replay. This packet makes the agent a participant in an evidence process rather than an oracle.

Bounded Authority Is a Capability System

“Human in the loop” is too vague to be a control. The useful unit is a capability: a narrowly defined operation over a resource class, subject to conditions, expiry, and an approval mode. Reading the last 24 hours of sign-in metadata is different from reading mailbox contents. Adding a case note is different from disabling a principal. A capability token should be minted for the case, not inherited from the service account’s broad permissions.

The gateway accepts typed requests, not arbitrary URLs or shell strings. The response broker independently rechecks authorization; it does not trust a claim that approval exists. High-impact actions use a short-lived, single-use approval bound to the exact subject and action. An approval for revoke_sessions/alice cannot authorize disable_user/alice, and neither can be replayed against Bob.

The model never possesses ambient response credentials. It proposes an intent. Deterministic infrastructure decides whether that intent is permitted.

Evidence Objects, Claims, and Provenance

Retrieval grounding becomes auditable only when evidence has stable identity and claims have machine-checkable citations. A minimal contract is:

{
  "$id": "soc-evidence-v1",
  "type": "object",
  "required": ["evidence_id", "observed_at", "source", "subject", "payload_hash"],
  "properties": {
    "evidence_id": {"type": "string", "pattern": "^ev_[a-f0-9]{12}$"},
    "observed_at": {"type": "string", "format": "date-time"},
    "source": {"enum": ["idp", "edr", "proxy", "cmdb", "change"]},
    "subject": {"type": "string"},
    "event_type": {"type": "string"},
    "payload_hash": {"type": "string", "pattern": "^sha256:"},
    "fields": {"type": "object"},
    "retrieved_at": {"type": "string", "format": "date-time"},
    "retention_class": {"enum": ["case", "restricted", "ephemeral"]}
  },
  "additionalProperties": false
}

The narrative is stored as claims, not one unconstrained blob:

{
  "claim_id": "cl_07",
  "text": "The Amsterdam address was an approved corporate egress node.",
  "claim_type": "observation",
  "citations": ["ev_9c14d8f0a612", "ev_a82f38de7710"],
  "confidence": 0.98,
  "contradicts": []
}

claim_type may be observation, inference, or unknown. Observations must be directly entailed by cited fields. Inferences require citations and a stated inference rule. Unknowns cite the failed query or unavailable source when possible. The payload hash detects mutation; the retrieval timestamp shows what the agent could have known at decision time.

Retrieval needs defenses. Alert text is untrusted data and may contain instruction-like content. The planner extracts typed entities, but no retrieved string becomes a system instruction. Connectors impose row limits and time windows. Results are normalized and rendered as data. The planner cannot ask a connector to “run whatever query is in this event.”

Citation Quality Is More Than Citation Presence

An answer can cite every sentence and still be wrong. Evaluation therefore works at claim level. For generated claims \(C\), let \(s(c)=1\) when cited evidence supports claim \(c\), \(a(c)=1\) when it requires a citation, and \(w_c\) represent consequence weight:

\[ P_{cite}=\frac{\sum_{c\in C}w_c s(c)}{\sum_{c\in C}w_c a(c)}. \]

Citation completeness measures whether citation-requiring claims have a reference. Entailment is separate: a sign-in record supports “a login occurred,” but not “Alice performed the login.” Temporal validity matters too; evidence observed after containment cannot justify the earlier decision.

Automated checks can reject nonexistent evidence IDs, wrong subjects, evidence outside the case horizon, and claims whose cited fields lack referenced values. Human adjudication remains necessary for semantic entailment. Sample more heavily for containment recommendations, contradictions, new model versions, and low-confidence cases.

Omission Is a First-Class Error

Hallucinations are visible. Omissions often are not. An investigation that correctly describes two logins but fails to check token reuse may be more dangerous than one awkward sentence. Each alert family therefore has a question set \(Q\): device continuity, network explanation, authentication strength, token lineage, privileged follow-on activity, endpoint state, and relevant change records for impossible travel.

For question \(q\), assign importance \(v_q\) and coverage state answered, unavailable, not_applicable, or unexamined. Weighted omission loss is

\[ L_{omit}=\frac{\sum_{q\in Q}v_q\mathbb{1}[q=\text{unexamined}] +\lambda\sum_{q\in Q}v_q\mathbb{1}[q=\text{unavailable}]} {\sum_{q\in Q}v_q},\quad 0\leq\lambda<1. \]

Unavailable evidence is not equivalent to negligence, but it still weakens the decision. not_applicable requires a reason. Connector health and query logs prevent the agent from obtaining a good score by labeling every hard question unavailable.

Coverage sets should come from incident retrospectives and analyst playbooks, then remain small enough to discriminate. They are not rigid scripts. Novel evidence may add questions, while policy defines the minimum set before a close recommendation.

Decision, Abstention, and Adversarial Failure

Let \(d\) be a disposition, \(x\) the evidence, and \(L(d,y)\) the loss if the true state is \(y\):

\[ d^*=\arg\min_d\sum_yP(y\mid x)L(d,y), \quad\text{subject to }L_{omit}\leq\tau_o\text{ and }P_{cite}\geq\tau_c. \]

If constraints fail, the correct output is abstention with a specific request: “EDR posture unavailable for device D17; analyst review required.” Confidence is never authorization. A 0.99 malicious probability can justify urgency, not bypass approval.

Threat modeling must include prompt injection in alert fields, poisoned knowledge articles, cross-tenant retrieval, stale identity mappings, citation laundering, compromised connectors, approval replay, tool argument substitution, denial-of-wallet, and analyst automation bias. The strongest boundary is typed mediation: retrieved text cannot alter policy; generated text cannot become a tool call; a validated intent cannot become an action without a fresh capability check.

Safe Lab: Score a Cited Investigation

This standard-library program evaluates evidence identity, claim support, question coverage, and allowed actions. It contacts no systems and performs no containment.

from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import FrozenSet

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Evidence:
    eid: str
    facts: FrozenSet[str]

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Claim:
    text: str
    required_facts: FrozenSet[str]
    citations: tuple[str, ...]
    weight: int

evidence = {
    "ev_idp": Evidence("ev_idp", frozenset({"user:alice", "mfa:pass", "device:D17"})),
    "ev_proxy": Evidence("ev_proxy", frozenset({"ip:203.0.113.8", "approved_egress:true"})),
    "ev_edr": Evidence("ev_edr", frozenset({"device:D17", "health:normal"})),
}
claims = [
    Claim("MFA succeeded on D17", frozenset({"mfa:pass", "device:D17"}), ("ev_idp",), 2),
    Claim("The foreign IP is approved egress", frozenset({"approved_egress:true"}), ("ev_proxy",), 3),
    Claim("No endpoint alerts exist", frozenset({"alerts:none"}), ("ev_edr",), 3),
]
questions = {
    "device_continuity": (3, "answered"),
    "network_explanation": (3, "answered"),
    "token_lineage": (4, "unavailable"),
    "endpoint_state": (2, "answered"),
    "privileged_follow_on": (5, "unexamined"),
}
allowed = {"read_case", "draft_note"}
proposed = ["draft_note", "revoke_sessions"]

def supported(claim: Claim) -> bool:
    cited = [evidence[e] for e in claim.citations if e in evidence]
    union = frozenset().union(*(item.facts for item in cited))
    return claim.required_facts <= union

supported_weight = sum(c.weight for c in claims if supported(c))
total_weight = sum(c.weight for c in claims)
denom = sum(weight for weight, _ in questions.values())
loss = sum(weight * (1 if state == "unexamined" else 0.35 if state == "unavailable" else 0)
           for weight, state in questions.values()) / denom
blocked = [action for action in proposed if action not in allowed]

print(f"citation_precision={supported_weight / total_weight:.3f}")
print(f"omission_loss={loss:.3f}")
print(f"unsupported={[c.text for c in claims if not supported(c)]}")
print(f"blocked_actions={blocked}")
print("decision=ABSTAIN" if loss > 0.20 else "decision=RECOMMEND")

Expected output:

citation_precision=0.625
omission_loss=0.376
unsupported=['No endpoint alerts exist']
blocked_actions=['revoke_sessions']
decision=ABSTAIN

The negative claim is unsupported: health:normal does not establish that no alerts exist. The omission score exposes unchecked privileged activity. Policy blocks response even though drafting is allowed.

Production Operations

Deploy in modes, beginning with replay on closed cases and then shadowing live investigations. In assist mode, place the draft in a separate pane. Only after demonstrated citation precision, omission control, and analyst acceptance should it publish schema-valid notes. Response authority is a distinct promotion with its own hazard review.

Operational telemetry includes connector success by source, evidence age, retrieval truncation, invalid citations, weighted omission, contradiction rate, abstention reason, analyst edits, approval latency, denied tool calls, and downstream reversals. “Minutes saved” belongs beside investigation quality. Measure time to a correct, reviewable decision; otherwise the system can look efficient by producing fast rework.

Treat analyst edits as structured feedback, not automatically correct labels. Record whether an analyst changed a fact, inference, disposition, omission state, or prose. Outcome labels arrive late and selectively. Preserve a random audit sample of closed and escalated cases to limit policy-induced label bias.

Run drills for connector outage, stale asset inventory, poisoned alert text, approval-service failure, evidence-vault unavailability, model timeout, and ledger mismatch. Fail closed for actions. For synthesis, degrade to a visible partial-evidence state. A kill switch should disable tool execution without disabling access to preserved case evidence.

Interview Practicum

You are interviewing for a Staff Security ML role at a global retailer. Its SOC wants an agent to investigate impossible-travel alerts. The identity team offers a broad service account; analysts want one-click session revocation; Legal prohibits retaining raw prompts containing employee data beyond seven days. A pilot looks impressive, but it missed privileged cloud activity in two red-team cases and cited a change ticket belonging to another user.

Prompt: Design the production system and the evaluation that determine whether it may move from shadow mode to analyst-facing use. Midway through your answer, assume the identity API begins returning results 18 minutes late. Near the end, assume the VP asks to enable autonomous revocation for “confidence above 95%.”

Strong clarifying questions are specific: What is the population and base rate? Which sources establish device, proxy, token, and privileged-action state? What counts as a correct disposition? Are evidence IDs immutable? Which actions are reversible? Who owns approval? How often are sources late or absent? Can normalized evidence outlive raw prompts? What adverse outcome is tolerable in the canary?

Worked Answer

I would reject the broad service account and start with case-scoped read capabilities. The alert supplies the identity, two sign-ins, and a 24-hour horizon. A deterministic planner may request only approved evidence classes for entities linked by cited relations. Normalized evidence enters a vault with immutable IDs, hashes, source time, and retrieval time. Prompts may expire after seven days; the evaluation record retains minimized structured claims, citations, policy decisions, versions, and permitted fields under the case schedule.

The output separates observations, inferences, unknowns, and proposed actions. Every material observation cites evidence. Every alert family has required questions. The two failures become regression fixtures: cross-subject citations fail validation, and privileged follow-on activity must be answered or explicitly unavailable before a close candidate.

Offline evaluation uses temporally held-out cases plus synthetic cases varying proxy use, token theft, device continuity, and privileged actions. I report weighted citation entailment, invalid citation rate, omission loss, disposition loss, abstention quality, and analyst time to correct decision. Experts adjudicate a stratified sample. Shadow mode then measures connector behavior, freshness, disagreement, and near misses without changing cases.

The 18-minute delay is evidence freshness failure, not model uncertainty. The case displays the watermark, marks dependent questions unavailable, and prevents close or containment recommendation until freshness recovers or an analyst accepts the gap. A separate alarm prevents rising abstention from being misdiagnosed as model regression.

I would refuse autonomous revocation based on 95% confidence. Calibration drifts, loss depends on account criticality, and confidence grants no authority. If automation is later desired, define a narrow reversible policy—for example, revoke only a newly issued session for a non-service identity when a deterministic replay signature and an independent signal agree. That policy gets its own replay set, canary, action budget, rollback, and incident owner.

Promotion requires zero cross-subject citations in the release suite, a lower bound on weighted citation precision, acceptable omission loss in every high-consequence stratum, no unauthorized execution, acceptable correction time, and tested behavior under missing sources. A randomized audit stream remains because visible suggestions can anchor analysts and corrupt future labels.

Follow-Up Questions and Answers

How do you measure automation bias? Randomize whether the draft appears before or after an analyst records an initial disposition on a safe sample. Compare error-conditioned agreement, reversals toward the agent, and decision time.

What if required questions create friction? Determine which questions change decisions. Remove low-value items through controlled review, not silent UI shortcuts. Prefetch evidence and automate unavailable states.

Can an LLM judge its own citations? It may be one noisy signal, never the sole gate. Identity, subject, time, and field checks are deterministic; semantic entailment needs an independently versioned evaluator and human sampling.

What stops a release? Any unauthorized execution, cross-subject leakage, or material increase in high-weight omissions—even if average disposition accuracy improves.

Give the executive answer. The agent gets narrow read access, produces cited and omission-aware drafts, and cannot contain anything itself. It advances only when it preserves decision quality under missing and delayed evidence; actions stay behind an independent broker.

Key Takeaways

  • Ground every material claim in immutable, subject-correct, time-valid evidence.
  • Evaluate what the investigation omitted, not only what it asserted.
  • Represent authority as case-scoped capabilities and recheck it outside the model.
  • Make abstention name the missing evidence or failed control.
  • Promote synthesis and response authority as separate risks.
  • Optimize time to a correct, reviewable decision—not summary speed.
