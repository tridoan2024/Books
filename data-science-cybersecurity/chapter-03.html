Chapter 3: Probability, Statistics, and Uncertainty

A malware detector advertises 99% sensitivity and 99% specificity. The SOC hears “99% accurate.” At a base rate of one malicious file per thousand, however, most alerts are false. Probability supplies the missing denominator; statistics tells us how uncertain our measurements are; simulation shows what the queue may look like tomorrow.

This chapter is organized as a quantitative case file. We derive the alert calculation, examine distributions behind security counts and delays, construct intervals, test a policy change, perform a Bayesian update, and simulate operational load.

Case File A: The Alert That Sounds Certain

Let (M) denote malicious and (+) a positive detector result. Suppose

\[ P(M)=0.001,\quad P(+\mid M)=0.99,\quad P(-\mid \neg M)=0.99. \]

The false-positive rate is (P(+\mid\neg M)=0.01). Bayes’ theorem gives

\[ P(M\mid +)=\frac{P(+\mid M)P(M)}{P(+\mid M)P(M)+P(+\mid\neg M)P(\neg M)}. \]

Substitution yields

\[ \frac{0.99(0.001)}{0.99(0.001)+0.01(0.999)} =\frac{0.00099}{0.01098}=0.09016. \]

So an alert has about 9.0% posterior probability of malware. In one million files, the expected confusion matrix is 990 true alerts, 10 missed malware files, 9,990 false alerts, and 989,010 true negatives. Analysts receive 10,980 alerts; roughly eleven out of twelve are false.

The detector is not broken. The verbal summary was. Sensitivity and specificity condition on truth; an analyst needs probability of truth conditioned on the alert. Base rates connect them.

Odds form and sequential evidence

Bayes is often clearer in odds. Prior odds are

\[ O(M)=\frac{0.001}{0.999}=0.001001. \]

The positive likelihood ratio is (LR^+=0.99/0.01=99). Posterior odds equal prior odds times likelihood ratio:

\[ O(M\mid +)=0.001001\times99=0.099099. \]

Converting odds to probability gives (0.099099/(1+0.099099)=0.09016).

If an independent sandbox result has (LR^+=20), posterior odds become (1.98198), or probability (1.98198/2.98198=0.6647). Independence is a substantive assumption. Two engines trained on the same corpus and signatures may provide correlated evidence; multiplying their likelihood ratios overstates certainty.

Random Variables Behind Security Telemetry

A random variable maps uncertain outcomes to values. For Bernoulli (X\in\{0,1\}) with success probability (p),

\[ E[X]=p,\qquad Var(X)=p(1-p). \]

The sum of (n) independent Bernoulli trials is binomial:

\[ P(K=k)=\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}, \quad E[K]=np,\quad Var(K)=np(1-p). \]

It can model malicious files in a fixed batch when trials are sufficiently similar and independent. Campaign bursts violate those assumptions and inflate variance.

Poisson counts and exposure

For events arriving independently at constant rate (lambda),

\[ P(K=k)=e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^k}{k!}, \quad E[K]=Var(K)=\lambda. \]

If a tenant averages two credential-stuffing alerts per hour, the probability of at least one in an hour is (1-e^{-2}=0.8647). Real attacks cluster, so empirical variance often exceeds the mean. A negative-binomial model or hierarchical rate can represent overdispersion. Always include exposure: ten alerts among 100 logins differs from ten among a million.

Exponential waiting time

The waiting time (T) in a Poisson process is exponential:

\[ P(T>t)=e^{-\lambda t},\qquad E[T]=1/\lambda. \]

Its memoryless property rarely describes remediation perfectly: old vulnerabilities may be systematically harder, violating constant hazard. Survival methods can model time-varying hazard and right censoring.

Normal, lognormal, and heavy tails

The normal distribution is symmetric and useful for additive measurement error and sampling means. It is often poor for positive skewed latency or loss. If (log X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)), then (X) is lognormal with median (e^\mu) and mean (e^{\mu+\sigma^2/2}); the mean exceeds the median.

File sizes, response times, and incident costs may be heavy-tailed. A power-law tail satisfies approximately (P(X>x)\propto x^{-\alpha}) above a threshold. Do not declare a power law from a straight line on a log-log chart; compare alternative tail models and estimate the threshold. Heavy tails make sample means unstable and extreme scenarios important.

Expectation, variance, and covariance

Expectation is probability-weighted average. Variance measures squared dispersion. Covariance

\[ Cov(X,Y)=E[(X-E[X])(Y-E[Y])] \]

captures linear co-movement and depends on scale. Correlation standardizes it to ([-1,1]). Neither establishes cause. Correlated detections may share a sensor, campaign, or label process.

Case File B: Estimation from a Sample

Suppose auditors randomly inspect 400 detector alerts and confirm 44 malicious files. Estimated precision is

\[ \hat p=44/400=0.11. \]

A point estimate without uncertainty invites false precision. The naive Wald interval, (hat p\pm1.96\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/n}), performs poorly near boundaries or with small samples. The Wilson interval has better coverage:

\[ \frac{\hat p+z^2/(2n)\pm z\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/n+z^2/(4n^2)}}{1+z^2/n}. \]

For 44 of 400 at 95%, it is approximately [0.083, 0.145]. This is a confidence interval: over repeated samples, 95% of intervals built by the procedure cover the fixed parameter. It is not a 95% probability statement about this fixed (p).

A Bayesian model with (p\sim Beta(1,1)) yields posterior (Beta(45,357)). A 95% credible interval allows the direct probability interpretation conditional on model and prior. Similar numerical intervals do not make the philosophies identical.

Prediction intervals are different

A confidence interval concerns a parameter such as mean alert volume. A prediction interval concerns a future observation and includes process variation. If daily false alerts are Poisson with uncertain rate, tomorrow’s predictive distribution is wider than uncertainty about the mean rate. Staffing from a confidence interval for the mean understates queue spikes.

Bootstrap and robust summaries

For a complicated statistic, resample observations with replacement, recompute the statistic, and use the bootstrap distribution. Resampling must match dependence: sessions from the same account or days from the same campaign may need cluster or block bootstrap. Median and trimmed means reduce sensitivity to extreme times, but the choice must match the operational question; the tail may be exactly what incident response cares about.

Case File C: Did the New Rule Help?

Before a rule change, 120 of 10,000 reviewed alerts were confirmed malicious. Afterward, 162 of 10,000 were confirmed. The observed precision difference is (0.0162-0.0120=0.0042), or 0.42 percentage points.

Under the null (p_1=p_2), pooled proportion is (282/20,000=0.0141). Standard error is

\[ SE=\sqrt{0.0141(1-0.0141)(1/10000+1/10000)}=0.001667. \]

Thus (z=0.0042/0.001667=2.52), with two-sided (p\approx0.0118). At a predeclared 0.05 level, this rejects equality. It does not prove the rule caused improvement: time, campaigns, reviewers, and sampling may differ. Nor does significance establish importance. The change adds about 42 confirmed cases per 10,000 reviewed alerts; whether that matters depends on misses, queue cost, and displacement.

Power is the probability of rejecting the null at a specified true effect. Rare outcomes require large samples. Designing for an implausibly large effect produces an underpowered study. Running hundreds of segment tests inflates false discoveries; use prespecified hypotheses, family-wise or false-discovery controls, and independent validation. Exploratory findings should be labeled exploratory.

Permutation testing can compare groups with fewer distributional assumptions: shuffle treatment labels under exchangeability and measure how often differences are at least as extreme. If assignment was not exchangeable, permutation does not repair confounding.

Bayesian Update Lab

This program reproduces the base-rate calculation, Wilson interval, beta posterior, and likelihood-ratio update.

from math import sqrt
from scipy.stats import beta

prevalence = 0.001
sensitivity = 0.99
specificity = 0.99
posterior = sensitivity * prevalence / (
    sensitivity * prevalence + (1 - specificity) * (1 - prevalence)
)
print(f"P(malware|alert)={posterior:.4f}")

n = 400; successes = 44; z = 1.96
phat = successes / n
center = (phat + z*z/(2*n)) / (1 + z*z/n)
half = z * sqrt(phat*(1-phat)/n + z*z/(4*n*n)) / (1 + z*z/n)
print(f"Wilson95=[{center-half:.3f}, {center+half:.3f}]")

lo, hi = beta.ppf([0.025, 0.975], 1 + successes, 1 + n - successes)
print(f"Beta posterior mean={(1+successes)/(2+n):.3f}")
print(f"Credible95=[{lo:.3f}, {hi:.3f}]")

odds = posterior / (1 - posterior)
odds *= 20
print(f"P(malware|alert,sandbox)={odds/(1+odds):.4f}")

Expected output:

P(malware|alert)=0.0902
Wilson95=[0.083, 0.144]
Beta posterior mean=0.112
Credible95=[0.083, 0.144]
P(malware|alert,sandbox)=0.6647

The uniform beta prior adds one pseudo-success and one pseudo-failure. In production, prior choice should reflect historical exchangeability and be tested for sensitivity. A posterior is only as credible as its likelihood and data-generating assumptions.

Monte Carlo Lab: Will the Queue Overflow?

Analytical expectations hide operational tails. Simulate daily traffic, malware arrivals, detections, false alerts, and analyst capacity. A beta distribution represents uncertainty in the false-positive rate rather than pretending it is known exactly.

import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(20260820)
days = 100_000
files = 100_000
capacity = 1_150
malicious = rng.binomial(files, 0.001, days)
true_alerts = rng.binomial(malicious, 0.99)
false_positive_rate = rng.beta(100, 9900, days)
false_alerts = rng.binomial(files - malicious, false_positive_rate)
alerts = true_alerts + false_alerts

print(f"mean_alerts={alerts.mean():.1f}")
print(f"p95_alerts={np.quantile(alerts, .95):.0f}")
print(f"overflow_probability={(alerts > capacity).mean():.3f}")
print(f"mean_true_alerts={true_alerts.mean():.1f}")

Expected output for NumPy 2.5.2:

mean_alerts=1098.6
p95_alerts=1277
overflow_probability=0.302
mean_true_alerts=99.0

The expected queue is below capacity, yet overflow occurs about 30% of days because the false-positive rate is uncertain. Simulation is not prophecy. Vary prevalence, correlation, campaign bursts, sensor outages, and analyst service time. Model validation includes checking whether simulated components reproduce historical marginal distributions and dependence.

Sampling, Bias, and the Law of Large Numbers

For independent identically distributed observations with finite expectation, sample averages converge toward the expectation. More data reduces random error; it does not remove systematic bias. A million adjudicated alerts can estimate the precision of reviewed alerts tightly while saying little about undetected malware.

Selection bias arises because current policy determines review. Verification bias occurs when only positives receive definitive testing. Survivorship bias excludes destroyed or unenrolled devices. Measurement bias enters when engines share labels. Concept drift changes (P(Y\mid X)); prior-probability shift changes (P(Y)). Security data violate IID assumptions through campaigns, tenants, repeated entities, and adaptive attackers.

Random sampling from the target population is the cleanest remedy for prevalence estimation. Stratified sampling improves precision for rare or important groups when weights are retained. Cluster sampling may be cheaper but increases variance. Convenience samples require a narrower claim.

Statistical and Operational Significance

A tiny precision increase may be statistically detectable at massive scale but operationally irrelevant. A modest reduction in critical ransomware misses may be valuable despite wide uncertainty. Report absolute effects, relative effects, intervals, denominators, and translated consequences.

Decision makers also need robustness. Does the conclusion change if prevalence is half the estimate? If false-positive cost doubles? If one campaign is removed? Sensitivity analysis exposes conclusions balanced on fragile assumptions. “Not statistically significant” means the study did not reject its null; it does not prove equivalence. Equivalence and noninferiority require margins defined before seeing results.

Dependence Is Not a Footnote

Many textbook formulas assume independent trials. Security observations cluster within users, hosts, tenants, days, and campaigns. Ten thousand files from one outbreak do not provide the same information as ten thousand independent families. Ignoring dependence makes standard errors too small and evidence look stronger than it is.

Match analysis to the sampling unit. Use cluster-robust uncertainty when assignment is individual but outcomes correlate within organizations; random effects or hierarchical models when group variation is itself important; and block resampling for time dependence. Report the number of independent clusters, not only the row count. With very few clusters, asymptotic corrections can remain unreliable, so design-based randomization inference or conservative aggregation may be preferable.

Calibration uncertainty deserves the same care. Reliability diagrams built from tiny bins can look erratic or deceptively perfect. Report bin counts, use adaptive or prespecified bins, and complement plots with proper scoring rules such as log loss or Brier score. Recalibration can repair probability mapping under stable ranking, but it cannot recover missed features, selection bias, or a changed target. When prevalence shifts, intercept adjustment may help; when class-conditional behavior changes, fresh labeled evidence is necessary. Treat every numeric probability as a claim tied to a population, time window, and adjudication process.

Interview Practicum

The 99%-Accurate Detector

Prompt with live constraints

“Explain why a 99%-accurate malware detector overwhelms our SOC and propose a launch evaluation.” The panel adds: one million files arrive daily; prevalence is 0.1%; analysts can review 2,000 alerts; sandbox evidence is correlated with the detector; and fifteen business units have different base rates.

Worked Answer

Strong answer

Clarify whether “99%” means sensitivity, specificity, balanced accuracy, or overall accuracy and on which population. Using 99% sensitivity and specificity at 0.1% prevalence, expect 990 true alerts and 9,990 false alerts per million, so precision is about 9% and 10,980 alerts exceed capacity.

Do not multiply detector and sandbox likelihood ratios unless conditional independence is defensible. Estimate joint performance on a representative, adjudicated sample. Calibrate per business unit or with a hierarchical model because posterior probability changes with local prevalence. Reserve probability samples below thresholds to estimate misses and prevalence.

Evaluate at the 2,000-alert operating point: confirmed malware captured, false alerts, calibration, severe-family recall, time to action, and queue-age distribution. Compare confidence or credible intervals, not only point estimates. Stress-test campaign days and uncertain false-positive rates through Monte Carlo. Launch in shadow mode, then bounded routing with predefined queue and miss gates.

Technical answer

At capacity 2,000, ranking quality determines which alerts are reviewed. If the top slice contains 800 true and 1,200 false alerts, precision is 40% and recall is (800/1000=80\%), assuming 1,000 malicious files. That is operationally different from evaluating a fixed binary threshold. Confidence intervals should account for stratified audit sampling. Business-unit posterior probabilities use local priors; pooling can borrow strength but must not erase heterogeneity.

Weak answers

  • “One percent error means 10,000 mistakes” misses truth-conditioned rates and class composition.
  • “Accuracy is high, so add analysts” accepts a bad operating point without analysis.
  • “Run both tools and multiply confidence” assumes independence.
  • “The (p)-value is below .05, so launch” omits effect size, queue, and causality.
  • “No alert means safe” ignores false negatives and verification bias.

Follow-Up Questions and Answers

Follow-up probes with answers

How estimate prevalence when labels exist only for alerts? Draw a probability sample from all files, adjudicate with a reference procedure, and weight strata. Without negative sampling, prevalence is unidentified.

What if zero malware appears in a sample? Zero does not imply zero rate. For (n) independent trials, the rough 95% upper bound is (3/n), the rule of three.

Why not one global threshold? Different priors and costs yield different posterior risks and operating points. Calibrate and govern segment policies while guarding against unstable small samples.

How address multiple family metrics? Prespecify critical families, report simultaneous uncertainty or control false discoveries, and validate discoveries on fresh data.

Communication drills

Thirty seconds: “At 0.1% prevalence, 99% sensitivity and specificity produce about 990 true and 9,990 false alerts per million files—roughly 9% precision. Evaluate ranking at the 2,000-case capacity, audit below threshold, calibrate by base rate, and simulate queue tails.”

Five minutes: Derive Bayes, translate to counts, discuss dependent evidence, intervals, sampling bias, segmented calibration, capacity evaluation, and Monte Carlo stress tests.

A defensible conclusion states which assumptions dominate and which additional observation would most reduce decision uncertainty.

Key Takeaways

  • Base rates can dominate impressive sensitivity and specificity.
  • Use distributions whose assumptions match counts, waiting times, skew, and dependence.
  • Confidence, credible, and prediction intervals answer different questions.
  • Statistical significance neither establishes causality nor operational value.
  • More selected data reduces variance around a biased answer.
  • Monte Carlo simulation reveals queue tails hidden by expected values.
