Chapter 25: Anomaly Detection Without the Hype

Anomaly Detection Is a Search Strategy

An anomaly detector does not detect attacks. It orders observations by disagreement with a model of ordinary behavior. That distinction is operationally decisive. A payroll administrator opening a compensation export at quarter end may be statistically exceptional and legitimate; a compromised employee slowly reading the same repositories they use every day may be statistically ordinary. Anomaly detection is valuable when it shrinks an otherwise unreviewable search space, not when its score is mistaken for a probability of malice.

The object being modeled must be explicit. For identity-access activity, a row might represent one access, one session, or an identity-day. Those choices produce different questions. A point detector can rank an unusual access. A session representation can expose an unusual sequence. An identity-day representation can reveal a collective anomaly: no single event is remarkable, but their count, diversity, and timing are.

This chapter develops an honest benchmark for that setting. It treats unreviewed observations as unknown rather than benign, reserves analyst capacity in the threshold, and demands an explanation that identifies the reference population and the features responsible for the rank.

Four Kinds of “Different”

Let \(x_i\) be an observation and \(c_i\) its context. An anomaly score is a function \(s(x_i,c_i)\) whose numerical scale is model-specific. Four distinctions prevent many design errors:

  1. Point anomaly: the vector itself is remote, such as 400 repositories read in one hour.
  2. Contextual anomaly: the event is unusual given context, such as ordinary volume at 03:00 local time for a daytime employee.
  3. Collective anomaly: a set or sequence is unusual although its members are not, such as a low-and-slow scan across hundreds of resources.
  4. Conditional anomaly: the observation disagrees with an appropriate peer or personal baseline, such as a finance identity invoking a deployment API common among SREs.

For an identity-day vector

\[ x=(\log(1+N), H(R), f_{off}, f_{denied}, N_{new}, N_{countries}), \]

\(N\) is access count, \(H(R)=-\sum_r p_r\log p_r\) is resource-category entropy, and the remaining terms describe off-hours fraction, denial fraction, first-seen resources, and country diversity. Log-transforming counts prevents one bursty feature from dominating. Entropy distinguishes repeated work on one resource from broad reconnaissance. Context must include identity class and role; otherwise service accounts and humans contaminate one another's baseline.

Robust standardization is preferable when attackers or batch jobs can create extremes:

\[ z_{ij}=\frac{x_{ij}-\operatorname{median}(x_{\cdot j})} {1.4826\operatorname{median}|x_{\cdot j}-\operatorname{median}(x_{\cdot j})|+\epsilon}. \]

The formula supplies a resistant scale; it does not claim the feature is normally distributed.

Methods and Their Actual Assumptions

Isolation Forest repeatedly partitions features and gives higher anomaly scores to observations isolated by short paths. It works well on nonlinear tabular patterns and scales reasonably. Its score can change with irrelevant dimensions, sampling, and contamination settings. It cannot explain security meaning without an attribution layer.

Local Outlier Factor compares local density. In simplified form,

\[ LOF_k(p)=\frac{1}{|N_k(p)|}\sum_{o\in N_k(p)}\frac{lrd_k(o)}{lrd_k(p)}. \]

A value above one means \(p\) is sparse relative to its neighbors. LOF can find a contractor anomalous among contractors even when that group occupies a globally sparse region. It is sensitive to scaling and \(k\); novelty mode must be trained only on reference data when scoring the future.

Cluster distance is useful when stable modes exist—interactive employees, CI workers, backup services—but a small cluster is not automatically hostile. Autoencoders compress and reconstruct normal examples; reconstruction error is a representation error, not maliciousness. They become attractive for high-dimensional sequences only after simpler baselines fail. With incomplete labels, extra capacity often learns logging quirks more efficiently than attacker behavior.

An ensemble should combine ranks, not arbitrary raw scores. If \(r_m(i)\in[0,1]\) is percentile rank under method \(m\), use \(S_i=\sum_m w_mr_m(i)\), with weights fixed on a development period rather than tuned until remembered incidents rise.

A Concrete Identity-Day Contract

The feature table has one row per tenant, identity, and UTC day. Production systems should retain the time zone used to compute off-hours.

CREATE TABLE identity_day_features (
    tenant_id TEXT NOT NULL,
    identity_id TEXT NOT NULL,
    identity_type TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (identity_type IN ('human','service')),
    role_family TEXT NOT NULL,
    day_utc TEXT NOT NULL,
    access_count INTEGER NOT NULL,
    distinct_resources INTEGER NOT NULL,
    resource_entropy REAL NOT NULL,
    off_hours_fraction REAL NOT NULL,
    denied_fraction REAL NOT NULL,
    new_resource_count INTEGER NOT NULL,
    country_count INTEGER NOT NULL,
    feature_version TEXT NOT NULL,
    PRIMARY KEY (tenant_id, identity_id, day_utc, feature_version)
);

The raw event contract needs event_id, event and ingestion times, tenant, resolved identity, identity type, role family, resource, category, action, decision, country, source, and schema version. “New resource” must be point-in-time: first seen before the observation ends, never first seen according to today's complete history. Identity merges require a versioned mapping because retroactive resolution otherwise rewrites a benchmark.

Peer selection is part of the model. A defensible fallback hierarchy is (tenant, identity_type, role_family), then (tenant, identity_type), then a global identity-type cohort. Record which level was used. A score compared against 14 peers does not deserve the confidence of one compared against 14,000.

Choosing a Threshold from Capacity

The contamination parameter is not attack prevalence. It is often merely how many observations to rank as outliers. If the team can investigate \(B\) identity-days per day, the first operating point is the top \(B\), subject to deduplication and diversity constraints:

\[ \max_t \sum_{i:s_i\ge t}\widehat{V}_i \quad\text{subject to}\quad |\{i:s_i\ge t\}|\le B. \]

\(\widehat{V}_i\) is estimated review value, not necessarily incident probability. Reserve 10–20% of capacity for random or stratified below-threshold review. Without it, the organization labels only what the detector already likes and builds a self-confirming evaluation set.

With incomplete labels, report adjudicated precision at the top, known-incident rank, rank stability under resampling, lift over random sampling, analyst time per useful lead, and coverage across identities and peer groups. Synthetic attack injection tests whether the representation can express a behavior; it does not estimate production precision.

Safe Runnable Lab: Peer Context Versus Global Rarity

This standard-library lab creates ordinary identity-days and three review cases. It demonstrates why context beats raw rarity.

from collections import defaultdict
from math import sqrt
from statistics import median
from random import Random

FEATURES = ("access_count", "entropy", "off_hours", "new_resources")

def mad(values):
    center = median(values)
    return median(abs(v - center) for v in values) or 1e-9

def robust_distance(row, reference):
    total = 0.0
    for feature in FEATURES:
        values = [r[feature] for r in reference]
        z = (row[feature] - median(values)) / (1.4826 * mad(values))
        total += z * z
    return sqrt(total)

def fixtures():
    rng = Random(25)
    rows = []
    for role, count, entropy in (("engineer", 42, 1.7), ("finance", 18, 0.8)):
        for i in range(24):
            rows.append({"id":f"{role}-{i}", "role":role,
                "access_count":count+rng.randint(-6, 6),
                "entropy":entropy+rng.uniform(-0.15, 0.15),
                "off_hours":rng.uniform(0.0, 0.12),
                "new_resources":rng.randint(0, 2)})
    rows += [
      {"id":"fin-broad-read", "role":"finance", "access_count":35,
       "entropy":2.1, "off_hours":0.08, "new_resources":11},
      {"id":"eng-night-burst", "role":"engineer", "access_count":83,
       "entropy":1.8, "off_hours":0.91, "new_resources":4},
      {"id":"fin-quarter-close", "role":"finance", "access_count":70,
       "entropy":0.7, "off_hours":0.03, "new_resources":0}]
    return rows

rows = fixtures()
peers = defaultdict(list)
for row in rows:
    peers[row["role"]].append(row)
global_rank = sorted(rows, key=lambda r: robust_distance(r, rows), reverse=True)
peer_rank = sorted(rows, key=lambda r: robust_distance(r, peers[r["role"]]), reverse=True)
global_top = [r["id"] for r in global_rank[:3]]
peer_top = [r["id"] for r in peer_rank[:3]]
print("global_top", global_top)
print("peer_top", peer_top)
assert "fin-broad-read" in peer_top
assert "eng-night-burst" in peer_top
assert peer_top[:2] == ["eng-night-burst", "fin-broad-read"]
assert len(rows) == 51

The assertions are the contract: both planted behaviors occupy the first two peer-conditioned positions. Quarter close is rare but legitimate. Production should encode its calendar context rather than expect an algorithm to infer it magically. When comparing Isolation Forest or LOF, keep this fixture, temporal split, and alert budget fixed; otherwise workload is confounded with model quality.

Designing the Benchmark Before Choosing the Winner

An anomaly benchmark needs chronological partitions. Fit transformations and reference distributions on an earlier interval, tune neighborhood size or tree parameters on the next interval, and report once on a later interval. Random row splitting leaks the same identities, recurring jobs, and organizational calendar into every partition. If the intended deployment must score unseen tenants or new business units, add a tenant or organization holdout as a separate test; temporal generalization and organizational portability are different claims.

Build challenge slices that correspond to representation risks: low-and-slow access, burst access, new employee, role transfer, service-account rotation, quarter close, incident response, and telemetry loss. Planted anomalies should be created by transforming held-out benign fixtures rather than pasting an obviously extreme constant. For example, preserve a user's daily count while increasing resource diversity, or distribute a burst across five days. Record the transformation recipe and never mix planted cases into estimates of real-world precision.

Stability matters because analysts experience rank, not an abstract objective. Bootstrap the reference period or refit across random seeds, then measure top-\(B\) Jaccard overlap and rank variance. A method whose top 40 changes almost entirely after a harmless resample is difficult to operate even if it captures one extra known incident. Investigate whether instability comes from small peers, correlated features, or observations near the capacity boundary. A deterministic tie-breaker makes replay possible but does not cure statistical instability.

Evaluation also needs a candidate-generation baseline. Compare against simple policies such as first-seen sensitive resource, robust off-hours burst, and random role-stratified sampling. A complex detector earns production cost only if it discovers useful cases those policies miss at the same review budget. Report incremental yield: unique useful cases contributed after baseline cases are removed. This prevents an ensemble from receiving credit for rediscovering every obvious rule hit.

Finally, define case consolidation before calculating precision. Ten anomalous identity-days from one compromised campaign may create one investigation, while ten independent identities may create ten. Event-level precision can look excellent while drowning analysts in duplicates. Evaluate alerts, consolidated cases, and incidents separately; measure time to first useful lead and total review effort. The benchmark should reproduce the queue the SOC will actually see, including suppressions, grouping, and delayed enrichment.

Model retirement criteria belong in the benchmark as well. Retire or redesign a detector when its unique useful yield remains below the transparent baseline across mature cohorts, when peer coverage falls below an agreed floor, when explanations cannot be reproduced from retained evidence, or when operating cost exceeds the investigation value it adds. A detector should not survive merely because it once found an incident. Conversely, one quiet month is not proof of failure when attack outcomes are rare. Use several consecutive evaluation windows, uncertainty intervals, and challenge-set results.

Ownership should be divided clearly. Data engineering owns event contracts and freshness; detection engineering owns representation, tests, and release artifacts; the SOC owns case workflow and disposition guidance; privacy and legal approve purpose and retention; business-system owners explain legitimate exceptional processes. A monthly review should inspect false-positive clusters, below-threshold audit discoveries, cohort coverage, and recent organizational changes. Parameter changes need versioning and replay. Quietly moving a percentile threshold to meet a dashboard target destroys the comparability of every subsequent result.

The final operational artifact is therefore not a model file. It is a versioned package containing feature definitions, reference-window rules, peer fallback logic, fitted artifacts, capacity policy, explanation format, challenge fixtures, audit-sampling design, known blind spots, and rollback instructions. That package makes anomaly detection reviewable as engineering rather than mystical as “AI.”

Explanations Analysts Can Falsify

“Score 0.83” is not an explanation. A useful record says: “rank 2 of 27 finance identity-days; 11 first-seen resources versus peer median 1; entropy 2.10 versus 0.81; off-hours behavior ordinary.” It identifies the reference set and surprising dimensions and lets an analyst reject the result because, for example, a role transfer made the peer group stale.

For distance models, contributions are squared standardized components. For Isolation Forest, perturbation or SHAP-style explanations can be used cautiously, but correlated features make attribution unstable. For autoencoders, per-feature reconstruction error is descriptive, not causal. Include model and feature versions, window, peer cohort, fallback level, missing features, and comparable observations.

Production Failure Analysis

Peer poisoning. A compromised identity persists until its behavior enters the rolling baseline. Use lagged windows, cap each identity's influence, and retain a clean long-term baseline.

Population churn. Reorganizations change role families; cold-start users have no history. Fall back explicitly and avoid high-consequence automation on weak cohorts.

Batch-job domination. Service identities create extreme counts and teach a global model that human bursts are ordinary. Separate identity classes before fitting.

Score drift without threat drift. A client rollout changes event cardinality. Monitor raw features and semantics, not only score histograms.

Threshold probing. An adversary distributes activity across identities or days. Add collective and graph features; do not depend on threshold secrecy.

Feedback blindness. Analysts review only the top queue, so missed behaviors disappear from labels. Maintain randomized audits and incident-linked retrospective evaluation.

Explanation theater. Plausible attribution can conceal an invalid peer group or future-contaminated feature. Show provenance and reference cohorts first.

The safe failure mode is usually to keep ranking while removing automated consequences. If feature freshness or peer coverage breaches its contract, mark results degraded; never silently substitute zeros.

Interview Practicum

Scenario: The Queue Changes While You Design It.

Prompt. A SaaS company has 800,000 daily identity-resource events, 70 confirmed compromises from the last year, and capacity to review 40 cases daily. Design anomaly detection.

Ask whether a case is an event, session, or identity-day; how identities and roles resolve; whether confirmed cases omit quiet compromises; what analysts can do; and whether blocking is allowed.

Worked Answer

Propose a ranking service. Build point-in-time identity-day and session features, partition human and non-human identities, compare robust peer distance with Isolation Forest, and allocate 34 slots to ranked cases plus six to stratified audit sampling. Evaluate incident rank, reviewed precision, identity coverage, duplication, and analyst minutes.

Follow-Up Questions and Answers

Midstream constraint. Legal prohibits retaining resource names beyond 24 hours, and the SOC loses half its capacity.

Tokenize resources with tenant-scoped rotating identifiers and persist only approved aggregates: category counts, entropy, novelty indicators, and sensitivity. A rotation boundary must not masquerade as new resources, so novelty needs a lawful stable category representation or removal. Refit because representation changed. Reduce the budget to 20, preserve at least three audit slots, and deduplicate related identity-days into cases.

Second change. An executive demands that scores above 0.95 automatically lock accounts.

Reject the semantics, not the executive. The anomaly score is not calibrated compromise probability. Offer bounded controls: require an independent signal, apply step-up rather than lockout, or automate only known policy violations. If automation is mandatory, calibrate a supervised decision layer on adjudicated outcomes and retain abstention; do not relabel an unsupervised score.

Weak answers choose an exotic model before defining the row, call unreviewed cases negative, set contamination to incident prevalence, or promise explainability by naming SHAP. Staff judgment protects audit sampling under capacity pressure. Principal judgment negotiates the consequence boundary and privacy-safe representation.

Key Takeaways

  • Anomaly scores measure disagreement with a reference model, not maliciousness.
  • Observation unit, context, peer group, and timing determine what is expressible.
  • Compare methods at the same review budget and reserve below-threshold audits.
  • Explanations must expose cohort, surprising features, missingness, and provenance.
  • Require independent evidence before attaching high-consequence controls.
