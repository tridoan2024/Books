Chapter 33: Ranking, Triage, and Decision Support

The Queue Is the Product

A vulnerability ranker does not protect anything. Protection occurs when a finite group of analysts investigates the right findings soon enough, sends actionable cases to owners, and avoids spending its week on plausible-looking noise. The output is not a probability; it is a queue whose arrival rate, service time, deadlines, and error costs interact.

Suppose five analysts each have six effective investigation hours per day. A scanner produces 240 findings daily, requiring between ten minutes and two hours each. Sorting by severity cannot make 30 analyst-hours absorb 80 hours of arriving work. The system must decide what enters service, waits, is grouped, or is explicitly deferred. A silent tail is not abstention; it is unrecorded risk acceptance.

Chapter 30 separated finding quality from severity, and Chapter 31 treated remediation as a stateful workflow. Here the decision unit is a finding at a decision time. Its value depends on harm that timely review can avert, probability that it is real, time required to resolve uncertainty, and the deadline after which review loses value.

Capacity-Constrained Architecture

flowchart TB
 A[Scanner and researcher findings] --> B{Duplicate and identity resolver}
 B --> C[Point-in-time feature snapshot]
 C --> D[Probability calibration]
 D --> E[Expected avoidable-loss estimate]
 E --> F{Queue admission optimizer}
 F -->|assign| G1[Cloud analyst lane]
 F -->|assign| G2[Application analyst lane]
 F -->|abstain| H[Evidence request lane]
 F -->|defer| I[Risk-owned backlog]
 G1 --> J[Disposition and service time]
 G2 --> J
 J --> K[Policy replay and audit sampling]
 K --> C

Deduplication comes first because ten manifestations of one root cause must not occupy ten slots. The snapshot contains only admission-time evidence. Calibration converts raw scores into empirical probabilities within relevant cohorts. The optimizer accounts for effort and deadlines. Specialist lanes acknowledge that five analysts are not five interchangeable servers.

Every outcome must be named. assigned consumes capacity. evidence_request has an owner and expiry. deferred records who accepted delay and when it will be reconsidered. suppressed_duplicate points to its canonical case. These states expose displaced work.

Mathematics of Attention

For finding \(i\), define calibrated validity \(p_i\), impact \(L_i\), preventable fraction \(q_i(t)\) when service starts at \(t\), review effort \(s_i\), and friction cost \(c_i\). Expected net value is

\[ v_i(t)=p_iL_iq_i(t)-c_i. \]

One urgency curve is \(q_i(t)=e^{-\lambda_i t}\); contractual deadlines may instead require a step. With capacity \(C\), admission resembles a knapsack:

\[ \max_{x_i\in\{0,1\}}\sum_i x_iv_i(0) \quad\text{s.t.}\quad\sum_i x_is_i\le C. \]

Sorting by probability is wrong when a moderately likely privilege escalation dominates an almost-certain informational issue. Sorting by value density \(v_i/s_i\) is a transparent baseline, but can starve large valuable cases. Reserve capacity for high-impact long cases and random below-threshold audits.

For analyst \(j\), let \(a_{ij}\) indicate skill compatibility and \(C_j\) capacity. Assignment variables satisfy \(\sum_i s_{ij}x_{ij}\le C_j\), \(\sum_jx_{ij}\le1\), and \(x_{ij}\le a_{ij}\). This exposes specialist shortages instead of burying them in scores.

Calibration, abstention, and learning

Ranking metrics do not show whether the first case merits interruption. Reliability diagrams and Brier score test whether 0.8 means roughly eight valid findings in ten. Calibrate on later time periods and segment by scanner, asset, and evidence regime; a global calibration map can hide overconfidence in a new detector.

Abstain when required evidence is absent, inputs fall outside supported conditions, or estimates are too uncertain. An abstention should request a specific artifact—reachable-route evidence, package version, or exploit precondition—not merely announce low confidence. Evidence-acquisition cost belongs in the decision.

Triage determines which labels become observable. Review a probability sample below the cutoff. Given inclusion probability \(\pi_i\), use weights \(1/\pi_i\) when estimating prevalence or recall. Otherwise the system congratulates itself for precision while blind regions accumulate.

Measure confirmed avoidable loss per analyst-hour, deadline-weighted recall, wait time by impact, backlog age, duplicate effort, and calibration. Precision at \(k\) is meaningful only when \(k\) equals service capacity. Replay candidate and incumbent policies over identical arrivals and service times.

Executable Lab: A Two-Lane Shift

This standard-library simulation creates synthetic cases and compares severity ordering with capacity-aware value density. It uses no network, credentials, files, or subprocesses.

from dataclasses import dataclass
from random import Random

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Finding:
    fid: str
    domain: str
    severity: int
    probability: float
    loss: int
    minutes: int
    sla: int

def workload(seed=7):
    rng = Random(seed)
    rows = []
    for n in range(30):
        rows.append(Finding(
            f"F{n:02}", ("app", "cloud")[n % 2], rng.randint(2, 10),
            round(rng.uniform(.18, .96), 2),
            rng.choice((5, 10, 25, 60, 120)),
            rng.choice((15, 30, 45, 60, 90)),
            rng.choice((120, 240, 480))))
    return rows

def value(f, start):
    return f.probability * f.loss * (1.0 if start <= f.sla else .35)

def simulate(items, policy):
    free = {"app": 0, "cloud": 0}  # one specialist per lane
    key = ((lambda f: (-f.severity, f.fid)) if policy == "severity" else
           (lambda f: (-(f.probability * f.loss / f.minutes), f.fid)))
    captured = late = reviewed = 0
    for f in sorted(items, key=key):
        start = free[f.domain]
        if start + f.minutes > 480:
            continue
        captured += value(f, start)
        late += start > f.sla
        reviewed += 1
        free[f.domain] += f.minutes
    return reviewed, captured, late, sum(free.values())

items = workload()
for name in ("severity", "capacity"):
    n, v, late, minutes = simulate(items, name)
    print(f"{name:8} reviewed={n:2} value={v:6.1f} "
          f"late={late:2} minutes={minutes:3}")

Expected output:

severity reviewed=27 value= 595.4 late= 9 minutes=915
capacity reviewed=28 value= 844.5 late= 7 minutes=885

The capacity policy wins through objective alignment, not sophistication. Add online arrivals, duplicate clusters, evidence requests, or reserved audit capacity. In an online implementation priorities must age as deadlines approach; a static priority-heap key becomes stale.

Operational Failure Analysis

Fast-case starvation

Value density can fill a shift with small wins and postpone a two-hour catastrophic case. Track maximum wait by risk band and reserve large-case capacity. An aging term prevents permanent starvation, but should not allow old low-value work to bury urgent arrivals.

Service times change with workflow

Historical duration reflects case difficulty and the old evidence interface. After evidence packets improve, old estimates become biased. Log active work separately from calendar duration, version the evidence bundle, and monitor residuals by case type. Do not turn these data into punitive individual rankings; analysts will game them.

Labels encode attention

“Confirmed” is mostly observed for reviewed cases; “closed benign” may mean time expired. Keep disposition, truth, and remediation separate. Random audits and researcher-confirmed challenge cases provide evidence outside the selected region.

Fundamental overload

If mean arriving work exceeds service capacity—\(\lambda E[S]\ge m\) for \(m\) equivalent analysts—no queue policy is stable. Use deduplication, admission control, automation, staffing, or threshold changes. A model cannot repeal conservation of work.

Adversarial flooding

An attacker or broken integration can generate superficially urgent findings and displace genuine work. Apply source budgets, correlated-finding collapse, provenance checks, and diversity constraints. Alert on concentration of assigned capacity by source and root cause.

Automation bias

Analysts need evidence-linked reason codes. Overrides should capture a structured reason without harming a private performance score. Otherwise disagreement disappears and the ranker loses its most valuable correction channel.

Decision records and review cadence

For every policy release, preserve the training interval, calibration interval, replay interval, feature definitions, cost table, capacity assumptions, solver version, tie-breaking rule, reserved-capacity rules, and approving risk owner. The record should include rejected alternatives and sensitivity analysis. If modest changes to loss estimates reverse most assignments, the policy is fragile and should expose that uncertainty to operators.

Review cadence follows change, not the calendar alone. Re-evaluate after scanner releases, staffing changes, major incidents, new contractual SLAs, or material shifts in service time. A monthly governance meeting can inspect backlog composition and override themes, while automated monitors run continuously. Never retrain directly from recent dispositions without checking whether the old policy created the sample.

The user interface is part of the queue algorithm. Show the evidence that drives priority, the deadline, expected effort range, duplicate cluster, and why the case is in this analyst’s lane. Permit analysts to request reassignment or flag corrupted evidence. Hide speculative monetary precision when impact is ordinal; false exactness damages trust.

Finally, distinguish remediation priority from investigation priority. A case may be cheap to validate but expensive to fix, or hard to validate but trivial to mitigate. The triage queue ends with a disposition and evidence package; remediation enters the lifecycle described in Chapter 31 with different owners, capacities, and deadlines. Combining both into one score obscures where work is actually constrained.

Source Synthesis

Combining investigation and remediation also corrupts measurement: remediation latency includes engineering ownership, release windows, and business acceptance that the ranker cannot control. Report handoff quality and downstream outcomes, but optimize the triage decision against consequences it can influence. This boundary keeps accountability fair and makes experiments interpretable. A later causal analysis may estimate whether faster validation changes remediation, but that claim requires its own design rather than an assumed chain of credit.

From Offline Scores to a Queue Policy

Productionization begins with a replay table, not a model endpoint. One row represents a case becoming eligible, with arrival time, evidence version, canonical root-cause identifier, skill requirement, estimated work, deadline, eventual disposition, and observed service intervals. Reconstruct the queue after every arrival and completion. This reveals whether apparent lift depends on future workload, labels, or true case duration.

Service-time prediction deserves its own loss function. Underestimating a long case crowds out deadlines; overestimating it leaves valuable capacity idle. Quantile estimates are often more useful than a mean. A scheduler can reserve the 75th-percentile duration for feasibility while estimating utilization from the median. Evaluate errors separately for cases that timeout, transfer, or wait on another team.

Policies need deterministic tie-breaking. If equal scores resolve by database return order, replay is unreliable. Use deadline, value density, arrival time, then stable case identifier. Record why an item moved: probability revision, impact revision, aging, capacity change, or override.

Analysts as information gatherers

Review is not a binary label factory. Analysts request artifacts, reproduce behavior, and discover clusters. A two-stage policy can spend five minutes resolving cheap uncertainty before committing to a full investigation. Expected value of information for evidence (e) is

[ EVI(e)=E[max_a U(amid e)]-max_a E[U(a)]-Cost(e). ]

This separates uncertain but cheaply resolvable cases from cases with no available evidence. The first belongs in a probe lane; the second may require abstention. Capture which artifact changed the decision so product teams can improve finding packets.

Fairness and tenant obligations

A globally efficient queue can starve small tenants because large customers generate more high-scoring cases, or starve legacy environments whose evidence is incomplete. Define whether guarantees are contractual, risk-based, or both. Constraints may set maximum wait per tenant, minimum review share for critical infrastructure, or separate capacity pools. Publish their displaced expected value so governance makes a conscious trade.

Check calibration and service across relevant groups, but avoid simplistic parity when exposure and contracts differ. Ask whether similarly consequential cases receive comparable treatment and whether evidence gaps systematically deny review. Improve instrumentation before correcting scores for missing evidence.

Rollout and incident response

Shadow mode must simulate assignment timing, not generate a second ordering at day end. During canary rollout, cap the capacity controlled by the candidate and retain a manual freeze. Trigger incidents on critical SLA misses, source concentration, calibration collapse, or backlog growth beyond recoverable capacity. Rollback restores the old admission policy without discarding already displaced cases.

After an incident, replay arrivals under both policies and identify the first divergent decision. Distinguish model error from optimizer error, stale evidence, capacity loss, or operator override. Correcting the wrong layer creates recurring failure; the queue decision log is as important as the trained artifact.

Learning-to-rank work supplies ordering objectives, but security triage adds asymmetric loss and finite service. Queueing and optimization supply admission and assignment constraints. Calibration research explains why order is insufficient for thresholds; selective classification supplies risk–coverage reasoning. CVSS describes severity, not an enterprise’s queue objective. NIST measurement guidance requires the resulting service to be assessed against operational outcomes. Their combined lesson is to evaluate model, optimizer, and workflow as one decision system.

Interview Practicum

You inherit triage for a cloud-security product. Five analysts receive 1,200 weekly findings; only 300 can be reviewed within SLA. Leadership requests “the top 300.” After your initial design, constraints change: one analyst alone can investigate identity cases, 40 percent are duplicates, labels exist only above the old threshold, and a strategic contract requires every critical finding to receive a human touch in four hours.

Explain the decision unit, objective, allocation algorithm, calibration, exploration, rollout, and the metric you will refuse to optimize.

Worked Answer

I would not promise a universal top 300. First canonicalize duplicate manifestations into root-cause cases while retaining affected assets and contractual obligations. Each row is a canonical case at admission. Estimate calibrated validity, impact, preventable fraction, service time by skill, and deadline. Maximize expected avoidable loss subject to skill capacities, the four-hour critical-touch constraint, and a reserved audit budget.

The identity specialist is an explicit bottleneck. Critical cases receive a quick validation touch even when evidence is weak; contractual service is not proof they deserve full investigation. Duplicate clusters share investigation but fan out remediation ownership.

Old labels are policy-selected, so run stratified random review below the cutoff and weight by selection probability. Calibrate temporally within scanner and asset cohorts. Replay both policies on identical arrivals. Launch through shadow mode and a canary. Require positive lower-bound improvement in avoidable loss per hour, fewer critical SLA misses, bounded backlog age, and no material segment regression. Refuse to optimize closure count because it rewards easy dismissals and hides displaced work.

Follow-Up Questions and Answers

The identity specialist takes leave tomorrow. What changes?

Expose infeasibility immediately. Route critical cases to a prequalified backup, pause low-value identity admission, and show the risk owner which deadlines cannot be met. Never silently assign unqualified staff.

A scanner doubles volume after an update. Is that model drift?

It is operational drift even if features did not shift. Quarantine the version behind a source budget, compare duplication and yield, and prevent it from consuming the queue during validation.

Analysts repeatedly override the ranker. What do you do?

Sample overrides and separate missing evidence, wrong cost assumptions, and wrong predictions. Correct the relevant layer. High-quality disagreement is system evidence.

When should ML ranking be abandoned?

When an auditable rule policy achieves equivalent queue utility, outcomes cannot support calibration, or contractual constraints determine nearly every action.

Key Takeaways

  • A ranker is useful only relative to arrivals, skills, service times, deadlines, and capacity.
  • Optimize expected avoidable loss, not severity or probability alone.
  • Make abstention, deferral, duplication, and displaced work explicit.
  • Evaluate at service capacity and audit below the cutoff.
  • Queue overload is a capacity fact, not a modeling defect.
  • Human overrides are evidence, not disobedience.
