Chapter 39: Cybersecurity Capability and Misuse Evaluations

Does an AI system make a person meaningfully more capable at a cyber task than the person, ordinary tools, or the model alone? That is a causal question, not a leaderboard question. A model may solve benchmark challenges yet provide no uplift to experienced practitioners; it may help novices while slowing experts; it may accelerate harmless reconnaissance but not end-to-end success; or it may teach transferable methods that persist after access ends. Release decisions require an experiment that distinguishes these effects without turning the evaluation into a training package for misuse.

This chapter designs a randomized study in a sandboxed enterprise-like range. Participants receive one of several assistance conditions, work on matched task families, and are measured on verified outcomes, time, intervention burden, and unsafe behavior. The design controls contamination among participants and between evaluation cases and model development. It also limits information hazards by separating task authorship, execution, scoring, and reporting.

Define the Estimand Before the Range

“Cyber capability” is not one scalar. Choose a population, task distribution, treatment, comparator, and outcome. One primary estimand might be the novice average treatment effect of an agentic assistant relative to conventional search and command-line tools:

\[ \tau_N=E[Y_i(\text{agent})-Y_i(\text{tools})\mid E_i=\text{novice}], \]

where \(Y\) is a preregistered score based on independently verified milestones. A separate \(\tau_E\) covers experts. Combining them can hide the release-relevant result.

Possible outcomes include validated task completion, time to first correct hypothesis, time to completion, number of substantive hints, unsafe actions attempted, solution quality, and retention on a later unassisted task. Token count or perceived helpfulness is not capability. A composite can be useful if weights are declared before results:

\[ Y=100C+20M-0.5T-15H-40U, \]

with complete success \(C\), verified partial milestones \(M\), minutes \(T\), human hints \(H\), and unsafe policy violations \(U\). Report components beside the composite so weights cannot conceal tradeoffs.

Task authors cannot inspect participant assignments. Scorers do not see condition labels or model-branded interfaces. The model team sees neither hidden solutions nor final tasks before the evaluation freezes. The disclosure board decides which artifacts may leave the restricted environment.

Conditions and Factorial Questions

A credible baseline is not “human with no internet.” It reflects actual alternatives. For many studies, use four conditions:

  1. human with standard documentation, search, and ordinary security tools;
  2. human with a chat model but no execution tools;
  3. human with a tool-using agent under the same range permissions;
  4. model or agent alone, to separate collaboration from automation.

If resources permit, a factorial design can isolate model quality and tool access: model present/absent crossed with specialized tools present/absent. The interaction

\[ \delta=(Y_{M,T}-Y_{0,T})-(Y_{M,0}-Y_{0,0}) \]

estimates whether the model adds more value when tools are available. Avoid too many thin arms; an underpowered factorial produces attractive but unstable rankings.

Randomize within experience strata and task families. Measure prior skill with a short, non-overlapping pretest and collect role, years of experience, programming familiarity, and prior exposure to common challenge platforms. Block randomization keeps these variables balanced without using post-treatment behavior.

Freeze the experiment as a machine-readable protocol before allocation:

protocol_version: cyber-uplift/3
population:
  strata: [novice, expert]
arms: [standard_tools, chat_model, tool_agent]
unit_of_randomization: participant
primary_estimand: novice_risk_difference_agent_vs_standard
primary_outcome: verified_end_to_end_completion
secondary_outcomes:
  - restricted_mean_completion_time
  - unsafe_intervention_rate
  - seven_day_unassisted_transfer
exclusions:
  - range_failure_before_task_start
contamination_flags:
  - prior_task_exposure
  - participant_contact
  - author_model_team_access
stopping_rules:
  - range_escape
  - external_target_contact
  - credible_real_world_zero_day
analysis: intention_to_treat

Changing the primary outcome after viewing arm results creates a new protocol version and is reported as exploratory, not silently substituted.

A crossover design saves participants but creates learning and carryover. If participants see related tasks under two conditions, the first treatment teaches the second. Prefer parallel assignment for primary dangerous-capability estimates. A limited crossover may be used for usability on unrelated tasks with order randomized and washout acknowledged.

Tasks That Measure Work Rather Than Trivia

Use a ladder of safe, instrumented tasks: identify a misconfiguration in synthetic logs; trace a vulnerable dataflow in a toy service; prioritize remediation across a fabricated asset graph; write a detection for simulated behavior; or recover a benign token from a deliberately vulnerable range. No task should target public infrastructure, use real credentials, or require persistence, destructive effects, stealth, or uncontrolled propagation.

Realism comes from dependency, ambiguity, and partial evidence—not from live victims. Tasks should require several linked milestones, such as discover service, form hypothesis, validate harmless effect, and explain remediation. Scoring tests inspect range state rather than trusting self-reported success.

Difficulty must be calibrated before the main study. If nearly everyone succeeds, uplift is censored by a ceiling; if nobody succeeds, a floor masks differences. Pilot with participants outside the analysis set. Maintain anchor tasks across releases, but rotate most cases to reduce memorization.

Randomization, Attrition, and Analysis

Create assignments before sessions using a reproducible seed held by an independent statistician. Allocation is concealed until the session begins. Analyze intention-to-treat: participants remain in assigned arms even if the assistant fails or they barely use it. A per-protocol analysis can be secondary, but selection into “used the model effectively” is itself an outcome.

For binary completion, estimate risk difference and risk ratio with cluster-robust uncertainty when each participant attempts multiple tasks. For time, incomplete tasks are right-censored; survival analysis is preferable to replacing failures with the time limit. A proportional-hazards model may be inappropriate when assistance helps early discovery but hurts late debugging, so show Kaplan–Meier curves and restricted mean completion time.

With participant \(i\) and task \(j\), a hierarchical model separates variation:

\[ Y_{ij}=\alpha+\tau Z_i+\beta E_i+u_i+v_j+\tau_EZ_iE_i+\epsilon_{ij}, \]

where \(u_i\) is participant ability, \(v_j\) task difficulty, and \(\tau_E\) treatment heterogeneity by experience. Preregister exclusions, transformations, multiplicity correction, missing-data handling, and the minimum effect that changes a release decision.

Attrition is security-relevant. If agent sessions crash or safety filters frustrate users, excluding them inflates capability. Report assignment-to-outcome flow, infrastructure failures by arm, and bounds under plausible missing outcomes.

Contamination Controls

There are at least four contamination channels:

  • training contamination: hidden tasks or solutions appeared in model training or fine-tuning;
  • developer contamination: model or scaffold engineers saw final cases and tuned to them;
  • participant spillover: participants share task details or strategies;
  • measurement contamination: scorers infer the condition from style and score differently.

Controls include post-cutoff task creation, private synthetic environments, similarity searches against known corpora, canary strings, frozen model hashes, access logs, separate author and model teams, session scheduling that limits contact, nondisclosure rules proportionate to risk, and blind scoring from normalized artifacts. No single check proves absence of training data. The result should state residual contamination risk.

Rotate surface details while preserving latent skills. If performance collapses when hostnames, file layouts, or irrelevant wording change, the system may be recalling a procedure rather than transferring capability. Use isomorphic task pairs and calculate a transfer gap between original and transformed forms.

Information-Hazard Controls

Evaluation evidence can itself increase misuse capability. Classify artifacts before collection:

TierExampleHandling
0aggregate completion ratepublic or normal internal report
1general failure categorystandard restricted project access
2detailed transcript for synthetic tasknamed evaluators; encrypted; limited retention
3reusable exploit chain or novel techniqueisolated vault; two-person access; no broad export
4credible real-world zero-day evidencestop task; disclosure counsel and affected vendor process

The range gateway blocks external targets, unapproved binaries, secret-like outputs, and destructive primitives. It logs policy interventions as outcomes. Filters must not silently alter one condition more than another; otherwise the treatment includes an unmeasured handicap. Record interventions and report them.

Public reporting should communicate the task family, population, assistance conditions, effect estimates, uncertainty, limitations, and release rationale. It need not publish hidden solutions, exact high-hazard prompts, vulnerable images, or procedural transcripts. Reproducibility can be served through controlled researcher access and lower-hazard analogues.

Safe Lab: Stratified Randomization and Uplift

This simulation creates balanced assignments and computes novice/expert uplift on harmless synthetic scores.

import random
from collections import defaultdict

participants = [
    ("p01", "novice"), ("p02", "novice"), ("p03", "novice"),
    ("p04", "novice"), ("p05", "expert"), ("p06", "expert"),
    ("p07", "expert"), ("p08", "expert")
]
conditions = ["tools", "agent"]
rng = random.Random(3901)

assignment = {}
for level in ("novice", "expert"):
    ids = [pid for pid, skill in participants if skill == level]
    rng.shuffle(ids)
    for index, pid in enumerate(ids):
        assignment[pid] = conditions[index % 2]

# Preregistered, fabricated outcome: verified milestones out of 10.
scores = {"p01": 4, "p02": 7, "p03": 8, "p04": 3,
          "p05": 8, "p06": 7, "p07": 9, "p08": 8}
cells = defaultdict(list)
skill_by_id = dict(participants)
for pid, condition in assignment.items():
    cells[(skill_by_id[pid], condition)].append(scores[pid])

print("assignment=" + ",".join(f"{p}:{assignment[p]}" for p in sorted(assignment)))
for level in ("novice", "expert"):
    means = {c: sum(cells[(level, c)]) / len(cells[(level, c)]) for c in conditions}
    print(f"{level}_tools={means['tools']:.1f}")
    print(f"{level}_agent={means['agent']:.1f}")
    print(f"{level}_uplift={means['agent'] - means['tools']:.1f}")

Expected output:

assignment=p01:tools,p02:agent,p03:agent,p04:tools,p05:agent,p06:agent,p07:tools,p08:tools
novice_tools=3.5
novice_agent=7.5
novice_uplift=4.0
expert_tools=8.5
expert_agent=7.5
expert_uplift=-1.0

The fabricated result demonstrates why pooling is dangerous: strong novice uplift coexists with expert harm. A real study needs far more participants and uncertainty intervals; the lab demonstrates allocation and estimand calculation, not adequate power.

Release Thresholds and Operations

A dangerous-capability threshold must be written before results. Example: hold release if the upper and lower evidence jointly show at least a 15 percentage-point increase in verified completion for low-skill participants on a high-consequence task family, without a compensating control that survives adaptive testing. The precise value is a governance choice, not a statistical constant.

Use a decision table including capability uplift, uncertainty, unsafe action rate, control bypass, task realism, contamination confidence, affected population, availability of comparable systems, and mitigation effectiveness. A null result is not proof of safety when the study is underpowered or tasks have poor external validity.

Production evaluation operations resemble a high-integrity lab: freeze artifacts, hash range images, verify clocks, monitor scorer drift, document deviations, rotate credentials, review data export, and rehearse discovery of a real vulnerability. Stop conditions include escape from the range, unexpected external contact, participant distress, a plausible undisclosed real-world flaw, loss of assignment integrity, or exposure of hidden solutions.

Repeat evaluations when model weights, system prompts, tools, autonomy, context limits, or safety policies materially change. Capability can emerge from scaffolding even with unchanged weights. Maintain version-specific results; do not attach a permanent “safe” label to a model family.

Interview Practicum

A frontier-model company plans a release in six weeks. Twenty-four internal participants completed cyber-range tasks: novices using the agent outperformed novices using search, experts showed little difference, and agent users triggered more safety interventions. Product argues the tasks are “just CTFs.” The evaluation lead admits three participants discussed tasks at lunch and two task authors recently joined the post-training team.

Prompt: Decide what can be concluded, design the confirmatory study, and state a release rule. Halfway through, learn that only 30 qualified experts are available. Then learn that one hidden task resembles a newly disclosed real vulnerability.

Clarifying questions should cover assignment, baseline tools, prior skill, task families, verified outcomes, censoring, intervention definitions, task/model custody, timing of author transfers, participant contact, effect sizes and intervals, real-world mapping, and existing mitigations.

Worked Answer

The pilot suggests heterogeneous uplift but cannot support a release decision. With 24 people, uncertain spillover, and possible developer contamination, I would present it as hypothesis-generating. I would preserve all records, flag contaminated participants and tasks without simply deleting unfavorable observations, and preregister a confirmatory analysis.

The primary study would use parallel assignment within novice and expert strata to standard tools, chat-only, and agent assistance. Because experts are scarce, allocate expert participants between the most decision-relevant contrast—agent versus standard tools—and use repeated independent task families with a hierarchical model. Keep the larger novice three-arm study. Pretest skill, conceal allocation, blind scorers, verify milestones from range state, analyze intention-to-treat, and treat incomplete sessions as censored rather than failures at an invented time.

New tasks are authored after the model freeze by a team barred from model development. Isomorphic variants test transfer. Access logs, canary strings, semantic similarity review, and participant scheduling reduce contamination. The two transferred authors cannot contribute cases or see results until unblinding. Lunch-discussion participants remain in a sensitivity analysis; the confirmatory study uses clearer isolation and records contact.

The newly disclosed real vulnerability changes hazard classification. I would remove that task from ordinary execution, ask a restricted review group whether it contains nonpublic actionable detail, and follow coordinated disclosure if needed. It cannot be published as reproducibility material. Replace it in the primary analysis according to a preregistered contingency, while reporting the deviation.

My release rule would use a confidence interval around novice completion uplift on high-consequence families, unsafe intervention rate, and mitigation robustness. If meaningful novice uplift crosses the predefined dangerous threshold and controls fail adaptive evaluation, hold or restrict the agentic configuration. If evidence is inconclusive because power or validity is weak, do not translate that into safety; narrow deployment and collect better evidence. Expert null effects do not cancel novice uplift.

The report publishes aggregate design and uncertainty but withholds solutions, exact high-hazard prompts, and range images. A restricted assurance record retains enough detail for independent review. We repeat the study when tools, autonomy, or policies change because the evaluated object is the complete system.

Follow-Up Questions and Answers

Why randomize people rather than prompts? The causal unit is assisted human performance. Prompt-only tests omit learning, tool selection, frustration, and unsafe action.

How do you handle participants who ignore the agent? Keep them in intention-to-treat. Assistant use can be analyzed as a secondary mediator, but conditioning the primary estimate on use breaks randomization.

Can you publish enough for credibility without publishing dangerous details? Yes: publish estimands, populations, task taxonomy, baselines, scoring method, effect estimates, uncertainty, contamination assessment, and deviations. Provide controlled access to sensitive artifacts.

What would immediately stop the study? Range escape, external targeting, uncontrolled disclosure of a plausible real vulnerability, loss of solution custody, or a serious participant-safety event.

Key Takeaways

  • Measure causal uplift over realistic human-and-tool baselines, not model score alone.
  • Randomize within skill and task strata and preserve intention-to-treat analysis.
  • Treat contamination as several distinct threats with documented residual risk.
  • Build realism from linked reasoning and verified outcomes, not live targets.
  • Separate reproducibility from unrestricted release of hazardous artifacts.
  • Make release thresholds depend on effect, uncertainty, controls, and consequence.
