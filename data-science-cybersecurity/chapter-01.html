Chapter 1: Data Science as an Evidence and Decision Discipline

At 08:10 Monday, the identity team reports that confirmed account takeovers have nearly doubled: 145 last month versus 80 before. Login volume is flat. Fraud losses are up. The CISO asks, “Why, and what should we do today?”

That sentence hides different scientific jobs. Counting cases is descriptive. Locating the increase in recovery flows or customer cohorts is diagnostic. Estimating which current sessions will become takeovers is predictive. Estimating whether a new challenge would prevent compromise is causal. Allocating limited challenge capacity is prescriptive. Treating these jobs as interchangeable creates confident but invalid security programs.

This chapter follows the ATO problem from alarm to decision. Its structure is a sequence of increasingly demanding questions, each with its own estimand, evidence, and failure conditions.

The Monday-Morning Claim

An account takeover (ATO) occurs here when an unauthorized actor controls a customer account and the case is confirmed by a defined adjudication process. An alert is evidence, not the outcome.

Let (Y_i=1) when active account (i) has a confirmed takeover whose compromise date falls in month (t). The descriptive rate is

\[ R_t=\frac{\sum_{i\in A_t}Y_i}{|A_t|}, \]

where (A_t) is the population at risk. A numerator alone can manufacture a crisis when the customer base grows. A rate alone can hide a large workload. Report both, with label maturity.

Confirmed cases are delayed observations. If cases take nine days to adjudicate, comparing incomplete August labels with mature July labels biases the trend. A new customer-notification campaign may increase confirmations without changing compromise. The first task is a measurement audit: did compromise, observation, or adjudication change?

Population, unit, and clock

  • Population: consumer accounts eligible to authenticate, excluding tests, employees, and already locked accounts.
  • Unit: account-day for exposure, login session for challenge decisions, or account-month for reporting.
  • Clock: login, compromise, report, and adjudication times are distinct.

A row is an observation. A feature is available evidence, such as distinct networks used in the prior week. A label is the recorded training signal, such as an adjudicator disposition. A target is what a model estimates. An outcome is what happens in the world. Labels are fallible measurements of outcomes.

Five Questions Hidden in One Incident

What changed? Descriptive work

The descriptive estimand is the change in mature incidence:

\[ \Delta_R=R_{\text{current}}-R_{\text{baseline}}. \]

It says nothing about cause. Compute it by acquisition channel, account age, authentication method, recovery path, and telemetry coverage. Denominators must follow each segment. “Forty cases used recovery” is meaningless without the number of recovery attempts.

Description also audits data quality. A rise isolated to one adjudication queue may be workflow. A rise paired with lower sensor coverage may understate harm. This stage earns the right to ask harder questions.

Where is the mechanism? Diagnostic work

Diagnosis searches for explanations consistent with evidence. Excess cases after email changes and password resets suggest a recovery mechanism but do not prove that recovery caused the increase.

Compare compromised accounts with eligible accounts from the same period. Investigate releases, controls, marketing, bot traffic, and staffing. A field populated after support opens a case is a consequence of detection, not a precursor.

The output is a ranked hypothesis set with disconfirming checks: credential stuffing rose from residential proxies; a release weakened device binding; support improved confirmation; or one partner cohort was compromised. Each predicts a different observable pattern.

Which sessions will become takeovers? Predictive work

For decision-time evidence (X=x), the estimand is

\[ p(x)=P(Y^{14d}=1\mid X=x), \]

where (Y^{14d}) means confirmed ATO within fourteen days. A model may rank well without explaining cause. Device novelty can predict ATO even if forcing known devices would be harmful.

Prediction needs deployment-shaped data: features reconstructed at login time, outcomes matured for fourteen days, and temporal validation. Random row splits leak recurring accounts and campaigns. Accuracy is useless at rare base rates; calibration, precision at capacity, recall, and workload matter.

Would intervention prevent harm? Causal work

Let treatment (A) be a phishing-resistant step-up. For eligible sessions, define potential outcomes (Y_i(1)) with challenge and (Y_i(0)) without it. The causal estimand is

\[ \tau_E=E[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid E=1]. \]

Only one potential outcome is observed. Naively comparing challenged and unchallenged sessions is biased because current policy challenges riskier traffic. Identification needs randomization, a credible natural experiment, or explicit assumptions with sensitivity analysis. A phased randomized rollout can estimate intention to treat while retaining emergency overrides. A predictive score is not a treatment-effect estimate.

What should the system do? Prescriptive work

Prescription chooses actions under constraints. It uses descriptive facts, predicted risk, causal effects, friction, and capacity. A policy may reserve passkey challenges for sessions with high compromise risk and contexts where challenge is effective.

Constraints belong in the specification: at most 6,000 daily challenges; no SMS fallback for administrators; abandonment increase below 0.3 percentage points; human review for high-value transfers. The answer is a policy frontier, not an unexplained threshold. If challenge does not help stolen active sessions, higher predictive precision does not solve that decision.

Lab: Turn the Alarm into Evidence

Save as ato_evidence.py and run with the book environment.

from collections import defaultdict

rows = [
    ("July", "password", 60_000, 48, True),
    ("July", "passkey", 40_000, 12, True),
    ("August", "password", 61_000, 105, True),
    ("August", "passkey", 42_000, 14, True),
    ("September", "password", 63_000, 82, False),
]

monthly = defaultdict(lambda: [0, 0])
for month, cohort, active, cases, mature in rows:
    if mature:
        monthly[month][0] += active
        monthly[month][1] += cases

for month in ("July", "August"):
    active, cases = monthly[month]
    print(f"{month}: {cases} cases; {cases / active * 100_000:.1f} per 100k")

j = monthly["July"]; a = monthly["August"]
print(f"absolute rate change: {(a[1]/a[0] - j[1]/j[0]) * 100_000:.1f} per 100k")

selected = {(m, c): (n, y) for m, c, n, y, mature in rows if mature}
for cohort in ("password", "passkey"):
    jn, jy = selected[("July", cohort)]; an, ay = selected[("August", cohort)]
    print(f"{cohort}: {(ay/an - jy/jn) * 100_000:+.1f} per 100k")

questions = {
    "descriptive": "How much did mature ATO incidence change?",
    "diagnostic": "Which cohort and mechanism explain the excess?",
    "predictive": "Which sessions will be confirmed within 14 days?",
    "causal": "Would a passkey challenge prevent takeover?",
    "prescriptive": "Which sessions consume limited challenge capacity?",
}
for kind, question in questions.items():
    print(f"{kind:12s} {question}")

Expected output:

July: 60 cases; 60.0 per 100k
August: 119 cases; 115.5 per 100k
absolute rate change: 55.5 per 100k
password: +92.1 per 100k
passkey: +3.3 per 100k
descriptive  How much did mature ATO incidence change?
diagnostic   Which cohort and mechanism explain the excess?
predictive   Which sessions will be confirmed within 14 days?
causal       Would a passkey challenge prevent takeover?
prescriptive Which sessions consume limited challenge capacity?

The fixture does not prove passwords caused the incident. It establishes concentration and excludes immature September. The next analysis must test mechanisms and population composition.

Building an Evidence Program

First frame the claim: population, unit, time origin, outcome, decision, and estimand. Next preserve event, ingestion, availability, intervention, and adjudication times. Audit sensor coverage, identity resolution, adjudication delay, and sampling. Reproduce the headline before modeling. Select the minimum adequate method: a rate table may resolve the initial alarm; logistic regression may rank sessions; randomization may be required for intervention effect.

Validation must resemble use. Recreate future traffic, label delay, capacity, and attack campaigns. Deployment versions the eligible population, features, model, treatment rules, and override behavior. Monitoring covers denominator shifts, maturity, treatment compliance, calibration, and adversarial displacement.

Reproducibility means another qualified person can reconstruct the result from versioned code, immutable inputs or snapshots, environment metadata, and a decision log. Peer review should challenge exclusions, temporal joins, outcomes, and identification assumptions, not merely style. Ethical review asks whether collection is proportionate and intervention friction is fairly distributed.

Simplicity reduces assumptions and review surface, but does not excuse weak design. No model repairs an undefined outcome. No causal method repairs severe interference without modeling it.

Case Resolution: What Can Be Said

Suppose the excess begins six hours after a release stopped invalidating old recovery tokens. It is concentrated in password accounts completing recovery. Rollback reduces suspicious completions immediately, while confirmed outcomes mature later.

The defensible report says the mature incidence increase is real; most excess cases use password recovery; timing and code inspection implicate stale tokens; emergency rollback is justified by mechanism evidence and reversibility; takeovers prevented are not yet identified; and a passkey experiment concerns future policy, not historical cause.

This is more useful than “the model found root cause.” It tells leaders what is observed, inferred, actionable, and unknown.

Analytical Ethics and Review Gates

An ATO program observes intimate behavior: devices, travel patterns, recovery attempts, network location, and sometimes financial activity. “Useful for prediction” is not sufficient authority to collect a field. The team should document purpose, retention, access, and whether a less intrusive signal can answer the same question. Raw IP histories need not be exposed to every model developer merely because a derived network-change feature is useful.

Intervention burden also deserves analysis. Step-up challenges can disproportionately affect travelers, accessibility-tool users, shared-device households, and customers in regions where preferred factors are unavailable. This is not resolved by deleting protected attributes. Without carefully governed cohort evaluation, the team may be unable to discover concentrated harm. Access to sensitive cohort attributes should be separated from routine scoring and used under an approved evaluation protocol.

Peer review should stop an analysis when the claimed population differs from the sampled population, label maturity is ignored, availability time cannot be reconstructed, or causal language exceeds the design. A reviewer should be able to answer four questions from the artifact alone: What quantity was estimated? Which observations could enter it? What assumptions connect data to the claim? What result would reverse the recommendation?

The incident tempo does not remove these gates. It changes the level of evidence appropriate to an action. A reversible rollback with strong mechanism evidence may proceed before a precise causal effect is available. A permanent program that challenges millions of customers requires stronger evaluation. Match evidentiary burden to action scale, reversibility, and potential harm.

Diagnostic Stop Rules

Investigations can become endless correlation hunts. Establish stop rules before opening another dashboard. Stop expanding diagnosis when a mechanism is reproduced technically, its predicted telemetry pattern appears, a reversible mitigation exists, and remaining uncertainty will not change the immediate action. Continue outcome measurement afterward to quantify effect.

Conversely, stop model development and return to instrumentation when confirmation coverage cannot be estimated, principal resolution is unstable, or key source timestamps reflect batch load rather than availability. A sophisticated classifier trained under those conditions converts measurement failure into numerical confidence.

Maintain a claim register with status values such as observed, supported mechanism, causally identified, contradicted, and open. Link each status to evidence and date it. During a fast-moving incident, this prevents yesterday’s working theory from becoming tomorrow’s unquestioned fact. It also creates a clean handoff from incident response to the longer-lived prevention program.

Evidence quality should be visible in the decision memo. A mature rate from a stable denominator may receive a high grade; a mechanism supported by logs and code reproduction a moderate-to-high grade; an extrapolated treatment effect from another population a low grade. Grades do not replace argument. They force the author to state why one claim is firmer than another. Record dissent as well: if identity engineering disputes timestamp semantics, the unresolved dispute belongs beside the recommendation. This habit makes later incident review scientific rather than ceremonial and prevents an executive slide from flattening heterogeneous evidence into one unjustified confidence label.

Interview Practicum

The Incident Changes Mid-Answer

Panel prompt

“Confirmed ATOs rose from 80 to 145. Design the response.” Five minutes later: “Support launched a self-service confirmation form halfway through.” Later still: “You may challenge only 5% of sessions, and fraud will not randomize obviously high-risk traffic.”

Clarifying branches

Ask when compromise occurs relative to confirmation; whether both counts are mature under one rule; how active accounts changed; which releases and controls moved; how challenge eligibility is defined; what “obviously high-risk” means; and whether boundary traffic can be randomized. The support form threatens comparability. Capacity turns ranking into allocation. Safety restrictions change the experiment’s target population.

Worked Answer

Strong answer

Reproduce the increase by compromise month with a fixed maturity window and active-account denominator. Model confirmations as the product of latent compromise and observation. Backfill historical cases to estimate how the new form changed reporting delay and confirmation probability; report bounds if cases cannot be recovered.

Diagnose concentration using recovery path, factor, account age, device continuity, network, segment, and telemetry health. Tie discontinuities to releases. Keep association language until intervention effects are identified.

Define prediction as confirmed ATO within fourteen days and validate temporally. At 5% capacity, report precision and captured cases at that operating point, calibration by cohort, and workload.

Estimate challenge effectiveness only where randomization is ethical. Route obvious-high-risk traffic to safety action; randomize eligible boundary sessions. The intention-to-treat estimate applies to that boundary population, not automatically to forced-action sessions. Measure completion, abandonment, fraud, and displacement into recovery.

Recommend emergency rollback when mechanism evidence and reversibility justify it, but label prevented cases as pending. Launch a bounded challenge experiment only after calibration and data-health checks.

Worked detail

If 80 observed cases had 0.70 confirmation probability and 145 had 0.90 after the form, simplified latent counts are (80/0.70=114.3) and (145/0.90=161.1). The observed 81% increase becomes an estimated 41% latent-count increase. This is sensitivity analysis, not truth; constant sensitivity is a strong assumption.

For assignment (Z), receipt (A), and outcome (Y), report (E[Y\mid Z=1]-E[Y\mid Z=0]) as intention to treat. Assignment can instrument receipt only if exclusion, relevance, and monotonicity are plausible.

Weak answers

  • “Train XGBoost on all logins” ignores the changed observation process.
  • “Block more because cases rose 81%” ignores denominators and maturity.
  • “Device novelty caused ATO” mistakes prediction for cause.
  • “A/B test everyone” ignores safety constraints.
  • “Never experiment in security” leaves ethical policy effects unknowable.

Follow-Up Questions and Answers

Follow-up probes with answers

Cases are selected by the current detector. What now? Adjudicate a probability sample outside its boundary and weight by sampling probabilities. Where withholding action is unsafe, give bounds rather than fictional recall.

Attackers adapt? Treat channel displacement as an outcome, time-index estimates, and refresh the threat model.

Passkey users differ from password users? Raw rate differences are not passkey effects. Require a credible counterfactual and state the overlap population.

When stop the experiment? Stop for predefined harm, data-integrity failure, abandonment, or overwhelming efficacy under a valid sequential design—not repeated ordinary (p)-value peeking.

Communication drills

Thirty seconds: “Separate compromise from changed confirmation, locate the mature denominator-correct excess, estimate fourteen-day session risk for the 5% capacity decision, and estimate challenge effect in an ethical boundary experiment. Keep emergency mitigation separate from claims about prevented cases.”

Five minutes: Lead with the four estimands: incidence change, concentration, session risk, and treatment effect. Explain observation correction, temporal validation, boundary randomization, capacity metrics, and what remains unidentified.

Key Takeaways

  • Description, diagnosis, prediction, causation, and prescription answer different questions.
  • Write population, unit, clock, outcome, and estimand before choosing a method.
  • Labels measure outcomes through an observation process that can change independently.
  • Prediction identifies neither historical cause nor intervention effect.
  • Capacity and safety are part of the scientific problem.
  • Distinguish what is observed, inferred, identified, and unknown.
