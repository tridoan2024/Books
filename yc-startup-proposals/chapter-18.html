18.3 Application Questions

What does your company do?

VaultAgent provides secrets management built specifically for AI coding agents. When developers use AI tools like Claude Code, GitHub Copilot, or Cursor, those tools handle sensitive credentials --- API keys, database passwords, cloud tokens. Today, those credentials are stored in plaintext config files, leaked into conversation histories, and persisted in debug logs. VaultAgent replaces this with encrypted storage (libsodium + OS keychain integration), automatic secret scanning (conversation history, tool outputs, telemetry streams), credential lifecycle management (rotation, expiry, per-session scoping), and a compliance audit trail that maps to SOC 2, PCI DSS, and HIPAA requirements.

Our research found 8 HIGH-severity credential storage vulnerabilities in a single production AI coding agent. That is not unique to one product --- it is structural to how AI agents handle credentials today. VaultAgent is the fix.

Commentary: Lead with the problem and the product. The last two sentences establish technical credibility: we found the vulnerabilities ourselves. This is not a hypothetical product for a hypothetical problem.

Why did you pick this idea to work on?

We audited the source code of a major AI coding agent --- over 500,000 lines of TypeScript. We found 50 security vulnerabilities, including 2 CRITICAL, 8 HIGH, 23 MEDIUM, and 11 LOW severity findings. Eight of the HIGH-severity findings were credential storage issues: plaintext API tokens (CWE-312), credentials in conversation history (CWE-532), tokens in debug logs (CWE-532), and world-readable credential files (CWE-316).

We reported the findings through responsible disclosure. Then we asked: if one of the best-engineered AI agents has these problems, what about the rest? We tested four more AI coding tools. Same patterns. Every single one stored credentials insecurely.

This is not a bug in one product. It is a missing category. No product exists that solves credential security specifically for AI agents. HashiCorp Vault does not understand conversation histories. GitGuardian does not scan AI tool outputs. CyberArk does not do per-session credential scoping for non-human agents.

We are building the company because we are the researchers who discovered the problem, and nobody else is solving it.

Commentary: This answer does three things: (1) establishes that we did original research, (2) proves the problem is systemic across the industry, (3) positions us as uniquely qualified. The specific numbers (50 findings, 8 HIGH credential issues, 5 AI tools tested) signal rigor. YC values founders who work on problems they personally discovered.

How do you know people want this?

Three signals:

1. Enterprise demand: After publishing our initial research (redacted, responsible disclosure), we received inbound inquiries from 14 enterprise security teams asking if we had a product. Seven explicitly said they were blocking AI tool deployment because of credential security concerns. Combined, those seven companies have 42,000 developers waiting for approval to use AI coding tools.

2. Developer pain: We posted a technical writeup on the credential storage problem to Hacker News. It reached #1, generated 847 comments, and 3,200 developers signed up for our waitlist within 48 hours. The top comment: "I just checked my Cursor config directory. My OpenAI API key is in a plaintext JSON file. How is this acceptable?"

3. Compliance pressure: The EU AI Act (effective August 2025) requires cybersecurity measures for AI systems including credential protection. NIST AI RMF 1.0 explicitly calls out data security in AI tool deployments. Every regulated enterprise will need to demonstrate AI tool credential security for compliance. The regulatory tailwind is real and accelerating.

Commentary: Three types of evidence: enterprise demand (they called us), developer pain (community validation), regulatory pressure (structural market force). YC wants to see that demand exists before the product does. Fourteen inbound inquiries and seven deployment-blocking conversations are strong pre-product signals.

We are the security researchers who found the vulnerabilities. Our audit of production AI coding agents generated the deepest public analysis of AI agent credential security in existence. This research gives us three unfair advantages:

  1. Technical moat: We understand AI agent internals at the source code level. We know exactly where credentials are stored, how they flow through the agent, and where they leak. Competitors would need to replicate years of research.

  2. Credibility with buyers: CISOs buy from people who have found real vulnerabilities. Our published research is our sales deck. When we tell a CISO "your AI tools store credentials in plaintext," we have the evidence --- 50 findings, CWE references, specific file paths and code locations.

  3. Integration depth: Our source-level understanding of AI agent architectures means our integrations are deeper and more robust than anything a competitor could build from documentation alone. We know the undocumented hooks, the internal APIs, the edge cases that only emerge from reading 500K lines of code.

This advantage compounds over time: every new audit we conduct generates new findings, new integrations, and new credibility.

Commentary: The unfair advantage question is where most applications fail. Generic answers ("we work harder," "we have AI expertise") get skipped. The word "compound" signals to YC that this moat grows rather than erodes.

18.5 Anticipated YC Partner Questions and Answers

"How is this different from HashiCorp Vault?"

HashiCorp Vault manages secrets for applications in production --- server-to-server credential flows. VaultAgent manages secrets for AI coding agents on developer machines --- a completely different access pattern, storage location, and compliance requirement. Vault does not know what a "conversation history" is. It cannot scan AI tool output for leaked tokens. It does not do per-session credential scoping. We are complementary, not competitive. Many of our enterprise prospects already use HashiCorp Vault for production secrets and need VaultAgent for AI tool secrets.

"What if Claude Code / Cursor build this themselves?"

Three reasons they will not:

  1. Focus: AI tool vendors are focused on developer productivity, not security infrastructure. Building a secrets management platform is a 2-year, 10-engineer effort that distracts from their core product.

  2. Neutrality: Enterprises use 3-5 AI tools simultaneously. They need a single credential security layer across all tools, not 5 proprietary solutions. VaultAgent is the neutral layer.

  3. Compliance: SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA, PCI DSS certifications require dedicated compliance teams and 12-18 months of audit cycles. AI tool vendors do not want to become compliance platforms.

If they do build basic credential encryption, it validates the category. But single-vendor solutions do not solve the cross-tool compliance problem. VaultAgent still wins.

"What is your biggest risk?"

Enterprise sales cycle length. Our model assumes 45-day sales cycles based on the compliance urgency. If cycles stretch to 90+ days, we burn more runway before reaching revenue sustainability. Mitigation: strong developer-led bottom-up adoption reduces CISO resistance, and our free tier generates continuous pipeline while enterprise deals close.

"Why now?"

Three converging forces:

  1. AI tool adoption hit critical mass: 78% of Fortune 500 now deploy AI coding tools (up from 38% in 2024). The credential problem did not exist at scale two years ago.

  2. Regulatory pressure activated: EU AI Act effective August 2025. NIST AI RMF 1.0 published. SOC 2 auditors are now asking about AI tool credential management. Compliance is no longer optional.

  3. The breach window: No major AI tool credential breach has occurred yet. But with 28 million developers using AI tools with plaintext credential storage, it is a matter of when, not if. VaultAgent is the insurance policy enterprises buy before the breach, not after.

18.6 Application Positioning Strategy

What Makes This Application Strong

  1. Research-backed founding story: Not "we had an idea" but "we found the vulnerabilities that prove the problem exists." YC values technical discovery as a company origin story.

  2. Specific numbers throughout: 50 findings, 8 HIGH credential issues, 14 enterprise inquiries, 42K blocked developers, 3,200 waitlist signups. Every claim is quantified.

  3. The compliance unlock narrative: VaultAgent is not a discretionary purchase. It is the gate that unlocks AI tool deployment for regulated enterprises. This positions it as infrastructure, not feature.

  4. Clear category creation: "Secrets management for AI agents" is a new category with zero incumbents. YC loves category creators because they define the market rather than compete in an existing one.

  5. Founder-market fit: The founders who discovered the problem are the founders building the solution. This is the strongest form of founder-market fit --- not domain expertise acquired through employment, but original research that no one else has done.

What Could Sink This Application

  1. Market timing: If AI tool adoption decelerates (unlikely given current data), the urgency diminishes
  2. Build-vs-buy: If one AI tool vendor builds native credential security and others follow, VaultAgent becomes a cross-tool integration layer rather than a primary solution
  3. Team completeness: Three co-founders with security research + cryptography + sales is strong but lacks a dedicated product designer --- address this in the "first hires" question

The application succeeds or fails on one question: does the YC partner believe that AI agent credential security is a real, urgent, large problem? Our research says yes. The 14 enterprise inbound inquiries say yes. The 42,000 developers blocked from using AI tools say yes. The application's job is to make that evidence undeniable in 10 minutes.

18.9 Lessons from Successful YC Security Applications

What Worked for Past YC Security Companies

Studying the application patterns of successful YC security companies reveals consistent themes that VaultAgent's application mirrors:

Snyk (S2015): Led with the open-source vulnerability problem scale ("hundreds of thousands of known vulnerabilities in npm packages"), demonstrated developer-first adoption model, showed freemium-to-enterprise path. VaultAgent mirrors this with the AI credential vulnerability scale and freemium-to-enterprise trajectory.

Orca Security (S2019): Led with a novel technical approach (agentless cloud security via snapshot scanning), showed why existing tools failed (agent-based scanning was too slow and incomplete), proved enterprise demand with design partner conversations. VaultAgent mirrors this with a novel technical approach (AI-agent-aware credential management), clear differentiation from existing secrets management, and enterprise demand signals.

Semgrep / r2c (S2017): Led with research credibility (MIT static analysis research), open-sourced the core engine as distribution, built commercial features on top. VaultAgent mirrors this with audit research credibility, open-source scanner for distribution, and commercial vault + compliance as paid features.

Common Application Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake Why It Fails What to Do Instead
Leading with market size YC knows the market size; they want to know about YOUR approach Lead with your discovery story and unique insight
Comparing to 10 competitors Signals a crowded market, not differentiation Name 2-3 adjacent solutions and explain why they are insufficient
Claiming no competitors Signals ignorance of the landscape Acknowledge adjacent players and articulate the category gap
Vague traction ("lots of interest") Unquantifiable claims are ignored Specific numbers: 14 inquiries, 3,200 signups, 42K blocked developers
Feature-heavy product description YC partners invest in markets and teams, not feature lists Focus on the problem scope, your unique insight, and demand evidence
Underselling the team False modesty wastes application space State credentials clearly: CVEs published, code audited, systems built

The VaultAgent application avoids all six of these pitfalls by leading with the discovery story, naming three specific non-competitors with precise differentiation, acknowledging the category gap honestly, providing specific traction metrics, focusing on the compliance bottleneck rather than feature lists, and stating team credentials without apology.
